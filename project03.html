PROJECT 03 · COLLAGEN FIBERS

Pigskin Collagen Extraction
& Wet-Spun Fibers

Optimization of ultrasound-assisted enzymatic extraction of type I collagen from pigskin, followed by wet-spinning fabrication and structural characterization of collagen fibers.

Collagen Ultrasound-Assisted Extraction Response Surface Methodology Wet Spinning Bio-based Fibers
01

OVERVIEW

From pigskin-derived collagen to wet-spun bio-based fibers.

Pigskin is a collagen-rich animal by-product and represents a potential renewable source for the preparation of high-value bio-based materials. Efficient collagen extraction and subsequent fiber formation provide a route toward the utilization of collagen in fibrous materials.

This project investigated ultrasound-assisted enzymatic extraction of type I collagen from pigskin. The effects of enzyme concentration, ultrasonic power, and treatment time were systematically evaluated, followed by process optimization using Box-Behnken response surface methodology.

The extracted collagen was subsequently processed into fibers through wet spinning. The influence of spinning conditions and collagen concentration on fiber formation, morphology, structure, and mechanical properties was investigated to establish a complete pathway from collagen extraction to fibrous material fabrication.

EXTRACTION & FIBER FABRICATION

An integrated pathway from collagen extraction to wet-spun fiber fabrication.

The research was conducted in two sequential stages. Ultrasound-assisted enzymatic extraction was first optimized to achieve efficient recovery of type I collagen from pigskin. The extracted collagen was subsequently used as the raw material for wet spinning to investigate fiber formation and mechanical performance.

PERFORMANCE & CHARACTERIZATION

Structural verification and fiber-property optimization from extracted collagen to wet-spun fibers.

Figure (a) shows the SEM image of pig skin collagen. Figures (b) to (f) respectively represent the scanning electron microscope images of collagen fibers prepared under different wet spinning concentrations (2.5%, 3.0%, 3.5%, 4.0%, and 4.5%).

The extracted collagen and wet-spun fibers were systematically characterized to evaluate molecular structure, crystalline characteristics, fiber morphology, and mechanical performance. The results confirmed the preservation of the characteristic collagen structure and demonstrated a strong dependence of fiber properties on spinning-solution concentration.

01

Collagen Structure

FTIR characterization revealed the characteristic amide bands of collagen, including Amide A, Amide I, and Amide II, supporting preservation of the characteristic collagen molecular structure.

02

Crystalline Structure

XRD analysis exhibited characteristic diffraction features of collagen, providing further structural evidence for the extracted collagen material.

03

Fiber Formation

Wet spinning enabled the transformation of the extracted collagen into continuous fibrous materials, demonstrating the processability of pigskin-derived collagen for fiber fabrication.

04

Fiber Morphology

Changes in collagen concentration affected fiber formation and morphology, with appropriate spinning conditions producing more stable and uniform collagen fibers.

05

Mechanical Performance

Increasing the collagen concentration substantially improved the mechanical performance of the wet-spun fibers, demonstrating the importance of solution concentration in fiber-property regulation.

06

Optimal Concentration

Fibers prepared from the 4.0 wt% collagen spinning solution exhibited the best overall performance, with tensile strength approximately 74% higher than that of fibers prepared at 2.5 wt%.
